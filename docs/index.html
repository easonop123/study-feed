The Merit gap

“I always get Achieved and I don't know why.”

Because Achieved is describing and Merit is explaining, and nobody ever shows you the difference on your own writing. Report comments say “add more detail”. That isn't a thing you can do. This is.

What you wrote — Achieved

“The rate increases because the particles have more energy and collide more.”

The same point — Merit

“The rate increases because the particles gain kinetic energy, so a greater proportion of collisions exceed the activation energy.”

Same idea. Same length. One of them names the mechanism. Study Feed marks your answer, tells you which one you wrote, and gives you the second version built from your own sentence — not a model answer copied off someone smarter.

The word that decides your grade

NCEA marks the verb, not the word count.

“Describe” and “Discuss” are different questions, and writing a beautiful description for a Discuss question caps you at Achieved no matter how long it is. Every long-answer card here is built around its command verb, and the marking checks whether you actually did what the verb asked.

Describe Explain Discuss Compare and contrast Evaluate Justify Analyse
Explain

Not “what happens” — why it happens. State the change, name the mechanism, then link it to the outcome. A description with no “because” is an Achieved answer wearing a Merit coat.

what does “justify” actually want
A decision plus the reasoning for it. Don't just say which factor matters more — say why the evidence points that way, and why the other one doesn't. Excellence usually lives in that second half.
