Why revision feels like guessing

There are three ways to not know something.

And they need three different fixes. Re-reading your notes only ever helps with the first one, which is why an hour of it can change nothing — you were already fine at the naming.

Achieved

You can't name it

The term, the process, the factor. You read the question and nothing arrives.

Flashcards fix this one. It is the only one they fix.
Merit

You can name it, but not say why

You know heating speeds a reaction up. You can't say that more collisions clear the activation energy.

This is where most Achieved answers actually stop.
Excellence

You can explain it, but not use it

You can recite the mechanism, then meet a situation you haven't seen and freeze.

Nothing you can re-read will teach you this.
Find my gaps asks you all three, in that order, and tells you which one you fall off at — then hands you the specific thing that was missing, not a score. Naming the rung is the useful part: “you can name it, you can't link it” is something you can act on tonight. “7 out of 10” isn't.

The Merit gap

“I always get Achieved and I don't know why.”

Because Achieved is describing and Merit is explaining, and nobody ever shows you the difference on your own writing. Report comments say “add more detail”. That isn't a thing you can do. This is.

What you wrote — Achieved

“The rate increases because the particles have more energy and collide more.”

The same point — Merit

“The rate increases because the particles gain kinetic energy, so a greater proportion of collisions exceed the activation energy.”

Same idea. Same length. One of them names the mechanism. Study Feed marks your answer, tells you which one you wrote, and gives you the second version built from your own sentence — not a model answer copied off someone smarter.

The word that decides your grade

NCEA marks the verb, not the word count.

“Describe” and “Discuss” are different questions, and writing a beautiful description for a Discuss question caps you at Achieved no matter how long it is. Every long-answer card here is built around its command verb, and the marking checks whether you actually did what the verb asked.

Describe Explain Discuss Compare and contrast Evaluate Justify Analyse
Explain

Not “what happens” — why it happens. State the change, name the mechanism, then link it to the outcome. A description with no “because” is an Achieved answer wearing a Merit coat.

Three ways to practise

Recognising an answer isn't knowing it.

Picking the right option out of four feels like knowing. In the exam there are no options. So the practice climbs: pick it, then produce it, then write it properly.

Find my gaps

The five-minute written test that tells you which rung you fall off, and what specifically was missing. One tap turns everything you missed into cards.

Learn

Takes a deck and drills it until you can produce every answer, not just recognise one. Pick it out of four the first time; write it from memory the second. Miss one and it comes straight back.

Quiz

A quick graded check before a test. The wrong options are matched to the right one, so you can't spot the answer by its shape — which is the only thing most quiz apps are actually testing.

When the card isn't enough

Ask, without leaving the question.

A tutor sits in the corner of every screen and can already see the card you're on, so “why is that the answer?” just works. Stuck on a past paper at 10pm? Photograph the question and ask about the photo.

what does “justify” actually want
A decision plus the reasoning for it. Don't just say which factor matters more — say why the evidence points that way, and why the other one doesn't. Excellence usually lives in that second half.
